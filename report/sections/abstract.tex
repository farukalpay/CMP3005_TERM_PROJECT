\begin{abstract}
\noindent MediDrone Istanbul must route capacity-limited drones from one laboratory to clinics on both shores of
the Bosphorus, where every crossing costs extra. We model the task as a capacitated vehicle routing problem
(CVRP), prove it \NP-complete via $\hc\le_p\tsp\le_p\cvrp$, show that even deciding whether the fleet suffices is
Bin Packing, and prove that the crossing penalty is a cut metric for \emph{any} shoreline. The cut structure gives
a Bin-Packing lower bound on the number of crossing routes --- attained exactly by our best plans --- and predicts
the penalty (1.12\,km) at which the routes over the real Bosphorus reorganise. We implement Clarke--Wright, a
giant-tour genetic algorithm with an $O(n)$ Split decoder and a memetic variant, and a granular tabu search with
$O(1)$ move evaluation and an adaptive capacity penalty, and confirm nine predicted growth exponents by
measurement. With equal time budgets, tabu search and the memetic GA reach the best-known value on the four
required CVRPLIB instances and on 72 and 71 of all 74 Augerat instances, the pure GA on 22; on the tightest
instance (load factor 0.992) only tabu search finds a fleet-feasible plan at all. Search trajectory networks in
partition space show why: only the methods that relocate clinics between routes funnel into the optimal partition.
Data include a real instance built from 905 OpenStreetMap health facilities.
\end{abstract}
