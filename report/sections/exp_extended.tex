\paragraph{74 benchmark instances} To test whether the conclusions survive beyond four hand-picked instances, all 74 Augerat A, B and P instances were solved with 5 seeds and 10\,s per run (\cref{fig:extended}a,b; per set in \cref{tab:extended}). At least one run reached the published BKS on 72 (TS), 71 (MA) and 22 (GA) of the 74 instances. A Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the per-instance mean gaps \citep{wilcoxon1945individual,derrac2011practical} confirms TS $\succ$ GA ($p=1.6\cdot10^{-11}$, $n=73$; TS better on 60 instances, worse on 0) and MA $\succ$ GA ($p=1.6\cdot10^{-11}$), whereas TS and MA are statistically indistinguishable ($p=0.218$; TS better on 5, worse on 12, equal on 56). \textbf{The hidden Bin Packing bites:} on P-n55-k15 (load factor 0.992) \emph{no} GA or MA run found a plan with at most $K$ drones, while every TS run reached the BKS --- walking through infeasible space is what makes TS robust when feasibility itself is hard (\cref{thm:bp}). Clarke--Wright violates the fleet limit on 25 of the 74 instances. On P-n55-k8 our 7-route solution (576) is \emph{below} the published 588, which assumes exactly $k=8$ vehicles; the project's definition allows at most $K$, hence the slightly negative P-set gaps.

\paragraph{Scaling to $n=1000$} On eleven X instances (\cref{fig:extended}c, \cref{tab:xset}; unlimited fleet, 60\,s) all methods lose quality as $n$ grows, but not equally: the mean gap is 0.95\,\% for MA, 1.51\,\% for TS, 3.91\,\% for GA and 5.13\,\% for Clarke--Wright, and MA beats TS on 9 of 11 instances --- on every instance with $n\ge250$. The complexity analysis explains the reversal: a TS iteration evaluates all $\Theta(nk)$ moves to apply \emph{one}, so at $n=1000$ it performs only about 100 moves per customer in 60\,s, whereas MA's first-improvement local search applies a move after $O(k)$ evaluations on average and Split re-optimises the whole partition at every decoding.
